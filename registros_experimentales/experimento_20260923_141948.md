# The Implementation of Carbon Taxes  
**Final Report**  

---

## 1. Introduction  

The global imperative to curb greenhouse‑gas emissions has led to the adoption of carbon pricing mechanisms worldwide. This study investigates the effectiveness of three distinct carbon‑tax structures—fixed, tiered, and variable—on industrial emissions and public perception. By combining quantitative emissions data with qualitative insights from stakeholders, the research aims to inform policymakers on optimal tax design and implementation strategies.

---

## 2. Objectives  

1. **Quantitatively assess** the impact of fixed, tiered, and variable carbon‑tax regimes on CO₂ emissions (tCO₂e) among industrial firms and residential households.  
2. **Evaluate** the influence of tax structures on production, pricing, and employment dynamics.  
3. **Measure** public perception and acceptance of each tax regime through structured questionnaires and semi‑structured interviews.  
4. **Identify** operational risks and propose mitigation strategies to enhance the validity and reliability of the experiment.

---

## 3. Theoretical Framework  

The carbon tax is framed as a policy instrument that internalizes the social cost of CO₂ emissions. Economic and environmental literature underscores that pricing carbon can stimulate emission reductions, foster clean‑technology innovation, and redistribute income. Case studies from Scandinavia, Canada, and China reveal mixed outcomes regarding competitiveness and income distribution. The study draws on:

- **Market‑based mechanisms** (cap‑and‑trade) and  
- **Public‑policy theory** (poli‑economics)  

to design an experiment that tests the efficacy and social acceptability of different tax structures.

## 4. Materials and Methods  

### 4.1 Population and Sampling  

| Element | Description | Source |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| **Target population** | Industrial firms, residential households, local authorities | Commercial registration database, national censuses |
| **Sample size** | 150 firms + 150 households per region | Stratified random sampling |
| **Regions** | Three socio‑economically similar regions | Selected to control for regional heterogeneity |
| **Treatment allocation** | 50 firms/homes per tax structure + 50 control | Randomized assignment with block stratification |

### 4.2 Variables  

| Variable | Type | Measurement |
|----------|------|-------------|
| **Dependent** | CO₂ emissions (tCO₂e) | Energy invoices, IoT sensors, gas inventory data |
| **Independent** | Tax structure (fixed, tiered, variable), tariff level, complementary subsidies | Experimental design |
| **Control** | Production output, price levels, employment | Monthly records |

### 4.3 Instruments  

| Instrument | Purpose | Format |
|------------|---------|--------|
| **Perception questionnaires** | Gauge attitudes toward carbon taxes | Paper/online standardized forms |
| **Semi‑structured interviews** | Capture nuanced stakeholder views | Audio recordings, transcription |
| **Energy invoices & IoT data** | Quantify emissions | Digital logs, sensor outputs |
| **Statistical software** | Data analysis | R, Stata, SPSS (institutional licenses) |
| **Qualitative analysis software** | Thematic coding | NVivo / Atlas.ti (not yet procured) |

### 4.4 Procedure  

1. **Define objective** – Evaluate impact of three tax structures.  
2. **Select samples** – Identify and randomize firms/homes.  
3. **Baseline data collection** – Record 12 months of emissions, consumption, and perceptions.  
4. **Implement treatment** – Apply the assigned tax for 12 months; provide educational material on revenue use.  
5. **Continuous monitoring** – Monthly emissions via IoT sensors; track production, prices, employment.  
6. **Final data collection** – Repeat emissions and perception measurements.  
7. **Statistical analysis** – Difference‑in‑differences (DiD), panel regressions with fixed effects, qualitative content analysis.  
8. **Triangulation** – Cross‑validate emissions with government records; verify questionnaire consistency.  
9. **Reporting** – Draft technical report, present to stakeholders, submit to peer‑reviewed journal.

### 4.5 Data Collection & Management  

- **IoT sensors**: 1 kW power, 0–10 tCO₂e/h range, LoRaWAN/5G connectivity.  
- **Data pipeline**: OCR readers for invoices, REST API ingestion, secure cloud storage (AWS/Azure).  
- **Security**: VPN, AES‑256 encryption, GDPR/LOPD compliance.
